TRANSFORM-2 esketamine trial (continuous endpoint)

Five-look Bayesian GSD on the MADRS mean-difference scale. Section 5.1 of the manuscript.

Setting

TRANSFORM-2 (Popova et al., Am J Psychiatry 2019;176:428–438) was a phase III double-blind, active-controlled, randomised trial of flexibly dosed esketamine nasal spray plus a newly initiated oral antidepressant against placebo nasal spray plus a newly initiated oral antidepressant in adults with treatment-resistant depression. The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in the Montgomery–Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS, range [0, 60]) total score at Day 28 of the four-week induction phase. The trial randomised 227 patients in a 1:1 ratio and reported a mean MADRS change of −19.8 in the esketamine arm versus −15.8 in the placebo arm, with a treatment-versus-control difference of −4.0 (95% CI −7.31 to −0.64, p = 0.020), establishing esketamine nasal spray as the first FDA-approved treatment for treatment-resistant depression.

We re-implement TRANSFORM-2 as a five-look Bayesian GSD (K = 5) on the mean-difference scale. The data-generating model has μc = −15.8 in the placebo arm and μt = −19.8 in the esketamine arm, with a common within-arm standard deviation σ = 13 on the MADRS-change scale (consistent with TRANSFORM-2's per-arm dispersion). The clinically relevant effect is Δ* = μt − μc = −4, with ΔH0 = 0. The fixed-sample design at α = 0.025 (one-sided) and 1 − β = 0.90 requires n = 222 patients per arm. The five-look GSD has looks equally spaced at n = 45, 89, 133, 177, 222 patients per arm, weakly informative priors μa ~ N(−10, 106) in both arms, a single-criterion efficacy rule 𝒫(Δ < 0 | ·) > 0.99 and a futility rule 𝒫(Δ > 0 | ·) > 0.90.

adabay call sequence

library(adabay)

des <- set_design(endpoint     = "continuous",
                  n_per_look   = c(90, 178, 266, 354, 444),
                  effect_scale = "mean_difference",
                  alternative  = "less",
                  sigma        = 13)

pri <- set_prior(endpoint = "continuous",
                 arms = list(c = list(family = "normal", mean = -10, sd = 1e3),
                             t = list(family = "normal", mean = -10, sd = 1e3)))

dec <- set_decision(
  efficacy = list(list(threshold_effect = 0, threshold_prob = 0.99)),
  futility = list(list(threshold_effect = 0, threshold_prob = 0.90)),
  futility_binding = TRUE)

oc_h0 <- evaluate_design(des, pri, dec,
                         effect   = list(mu_c = -15.8, mu_t = -15.8),
                         n_trials = 1e6, cores = 8, seed = 1L)
oc_h1 <- evaluate_design(des, pri, dec,
                         effect   = list(mu_c = -15.8, mu_t = -19.8),
                         n_trials = 1e6, cores = 8, seed = 1L)

print(oc_h1)
plot(oc_h1)

Operating characteristics

At R = 106 virtual trials per scenario the re-design reaches a type I error rate of 3.09% and a power of 85.59%, with an expected sample size of 265 patients across both arms under H1 (compared with the fixed-sample maximum of 444 across both arms).

adabay runs on 8 CPU cores via mclapply with the L'Ecuyer combined multiple-recursive generator (cores = 8); adaptr on 8 cores via its native parallel backend; gsbDesign analytic on a single core; BATSS with 8 mclapply workers and INLA pinned to one thread per worker. Type I error is evaluated at μt − μc = 0 and power at μt − μc = −4 (the observed TRANSFORM-2 mean-difference effect). All three simulation-based tools and the analytic gsbDesign reference agree within Monte Carlo error.

What this exercises

  • The normal–normal closed-form per-look posterior of the mean difference.
  • A weakly informative conjugate prior in its natural parameterisation, anchored at the placebo mean.
  • Binding futility and a single-criterion efficacy rule.
Reproduce. The full script is shipped with the package as inst/examples/transform2.R.
